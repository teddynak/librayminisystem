# Presentation and recording guide

The assignment requires a demonstration, oral presentation and a Google Drive video link explaining the project. No duration or slide count is specified. Follow any additional lecturer instructions you receive.

## Suggested recording sequence

1. Introduce yourself, registration number, course and project title.
2. Explain the manual-record problem and the single-library scope.
3. Show login and the librarian summary.
4. Register a title, add two copies and search by author or title.
5. Register a member and edit the member's name.
6. Issue a copy and show its active loan and reduced availability.
7. Return the loan and show restored availability and preserved history.
8. Log in as the member and show only that member's records.
9. Restart the application and show that the records remain saved.
10. Open the source code and explain classes, constructors, encapsulation, inheritance, overriding, overloading, abstraction, Searchable, collections, exceptions and JDBC transactions.
11. Run the tests or show the saved genuine test output. Distinguish automated headless tests from manual desktop checks.
12. State the limitations and conclude the demonstration.

Prepare a separate demo database containing fictional accounts. Never show your personal password or unrelated computer files in the recording. A test overdue screenshot can support the explanation, but identify it as test evidence rather than pretending the normal demonstration loan is overdue.

## Questions to practise

- Why are Book and BookCopy separate?
- Why is User abstract?
- What does getRole demonstrate?
- How do overriding and overloading differ in this project?
- Why does Searchable use a generic type?
- What happens if the loan insert fails after the copy update?
- How does the database reject two active loans for one copy?
- Why are copies made inactive rather than deleting their records?
- Where are member permissions checked besides the UI?
- How do you know the records persist after restart?
- Which tests actually ran, and which desktop checks remain?

## Submit the recording

Record your own spoken explanation, upload the video to Google Drive, and grant the lecturer permission to view it. Test the link with the intended access setting. Put the real link in your final submission message or document. The repository cannot supply your personal oral defense or create a Google Drive recording on your behalf.
